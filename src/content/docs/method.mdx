NeCast estimates the size history of a population from one window of genome sequence. It reads
the unphased genotypes of 32 diploid individuals in 30 Mb of accessible sequence, together with
the recombination and mutation maps estimated for that window, and returns log₁₀ N<sub>e</sub> on
64 time intervals evenly spaced in log time between 50 and 500,000 generations ago. A window takes
seconds on a CPU.

## Five streams over the same sites

- **Site encoder.** It reads the genotypes at every segregating site with each site's position on
  the recombination and mutation maps, so that distances count expected crossovers and mutations.
- **Site-frequency spectrum.** The folded spectrum of the 32 individuals.
- **Linkage disequilibrium.** Between sites 1 to 64 segregating sites apart, grouped by genetic
  distance.
- **Identity-by-state tracts.** The length spectra of tracts shared by the 496 pairs of
  individuals.
- **PSMC+ posterior.** The posterior of PSMC+ (Cousins et al. 2024), decoded for each individual
  with both maps and pooled over the 32.

The network outputs a correction for each of the 64 intervals and adds it to a baseline curve:
the window's Watterson estimate of log₁₀ N<sub>e</sub> plus a fixed offset per interval.

## Training

NeCast was trained on 89,996 simulated windows of the [simulated corpus](../data/simulations/).
Another 500 training windows chose the epoch, and 500 development windows are the test set. The
windows come from twelve families of size history. Most carry sequencing-like errors (genotype
errors, missing calls, false variant sites and phase switches), and most carry maps that vary
along the sequence, given to the network as estimated maps with estimation error. The training
settings and the record of every epoch are on the [trained network](../model/) page.

## What it assumes

<Aside type="note">
  - A window is 30 Mb of accessible sequence, and NeCast reads 32 diploid individuals from it.
  - Every simulated population mates at random. On real data, read the curve as an inverse
    coalescence rate (IICR), which equals N<sub>e</sub> only when that holds.
</Aside>
